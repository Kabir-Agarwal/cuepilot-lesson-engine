Chapter 7: Fractions — Class 4 Mathematics

What is a fraction?
When we break a whole thing into equal parts, each part is called a fraction of the whole.
If Meena cuts one roti into 4 equal parts and eats one part, she has eaten one-fourth of the
roti. We write this as 1/4. The number below the line is the denominator. It tells us how many
equal parts the whole was broken into. The number above the line is the numerator. It tells us
how many of those parts we are talking about. In 3/4, the denominator 4 says the roti was cut
into 4 equal parts, and the numerator 3 says we are talking about 3 of them.

Equal parts matter
A fraction is only correct when all the parts are equal. If a chapati is torn into two pieces and
one piece is much bigger than the other, the small piece is not one-half. Half means the two
pieces are exactly the same size. Ask children to fold a square of paper twice to make four
equal squares — each one is 1/4 of the paper.

Fractions on the number line
Every fraction has a place on the number line. To show fourths, draw a line from 0 to 1 and
divide the gap into 4 equal hops. The first hop lands on 1/4, the second on 2/4, the third on
3/4, and the fourth lands on 1. This shows that 4/4 is the same as one whole. It also shows why
2/4 lands on the same point as 1/2 — two of the four hops cover exactly half the distance.

Comparing fractions
When the denominators are the same, the fraction with the bigger numerator is bigger. 3/5 is
bigger than 2/5 because three parts of the same size are more than two parts. When the
numerators are the same, the fraction with the smaller denominator is bigger. 1/3 is bigger
than 1/6 because cutting a laddoo into 3 pieces gives bigger pieces than cutting it into 6.
Many children guess that 1/6 is bigger because 6 is bigger than 3. Use a picture or a paper
strip to show them the truth.

Equivalent fractions
Fractions that name the same amount are called equivalent fractions. 1/2, 2/4 and 4/8 all name
the same amount. We get them by multiplying the numerator and the denominator by the same
number. 1/2 multiplied above and below by 2 gives 2/4. On a paper strip, 1/2 and 2/4 cover
exactly the same length.

Fractions of a collection
Fractions also work on groups of things. If there are 12 marbles and we take 1/3 of them, we
first make 3 equal groups of 4 marbles each, and then take one group. So 1/3 of 12 is 4. In the
same way, 1/4 of 12 is 3, and 3/4 of 12 is 9.

Fractions in daily life
We use fractions all day. Half a glass of milk, quarter past four on the clock, half a kilogram
of sugar, one-third of a pizza. Ask children to collect five examples of fractions they saw at
home and share them in the next class.

Common mistakes to watch for
Children often forget that the parts must be equal. They also compare fractions by looking only
at the denominator. Some write 1/4 when they mean 4 parts of something. Slow, picture-first
work with paper folding and number lines fixes all three.
